# Fictional worked example: PageNest

This is an invented consumer product and a paper exercise. No app, capture, read-through, audience study or export is claimed.
The story text is fictional, including the book title and every chat message. It contains no customer testimonial.

## Shared brief B1

- Format: 35-second product demo for a sample-journal page; landscape, with captions and silent viewing support.
- Audience: parents who want to share family reading moments. Their current alternative is a book title in a message thread.
- Protagonist: a grandparent preparing the next bedtime story. Their goal is to remember where the family stopped.
- Proposition: keep a book note and the family's response together in one reading entry.
- Think/feel/do: “I can find the reading moment”; recognized; open the sample journal.
- Hypothetical customer language: “Which book did we finish?” No interview or quote evidence exists.
- Reason to believe: a visible entry with a book title, note and family reply. Main objection: “Another thing to maintain?”
- Answer: show one entry being added; do not claim measured ease or time savings.
- CTA: “Explore the sample journal.” Its destination must exist before any public use.
- Sources: fictional product assumptions F1 and fictional story F2 below. All UI is simulated and labeled throughout its display.
- Production: paper handoff only. No tool, generation, spend, installation or publication is selected.
- Direction: this exercise selects concept A. Real work needs the user's existing or delegated direction decision.

F1 assumes a product can add a book note and attach a family reply to its entry.
These are assumed capabilities, not verified functionality. Live capture is required before asserting that a real product performs them.
F2 permits the invented family scene and dialogue solely as fictional illustration.

## Three concept cards

| ID | Premise and mechanism | Proof, ending and feasibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: The next bedtime | Grandparent cannot remember the finished book; a shared entry resolves the question and prompts tomorrow's choice. Linear problem/action/consequence. | Entry and reply support the mechanism; end on the next book. Needs fictional scene and labeled simulated UI. |
| B: The skeptical parent | Parent asks whether this is another list; a direct demonstration tests the objection through one entry and reply. Question/test/answer structure. | Show only F1 assumptions. End with invitation to inspect the sample. Mostly deterministic UI layers. |
| C: The traveling book | The book narrates its journey between family homes, with each reading leaving a visible note. Object viewpoint and accumulated discovery. | Notes connect the journey; end on a shared reading memory. Needs more staged scenes and narration. |

A best serves the grandparent's immediate goal in this brief. B foregrounds the maintenance objection; C needs more scene changes.
These are editorial judgments, not evidence of comparative audience preference.
