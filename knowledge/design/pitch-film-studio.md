# Pitch Film Studio

Use this method for a consumer product's sales pitch, product demo, short ad, or brand story.
This is a writing and production method, not a runtime or a publishing service.

## Focused film method

Start from the requested deliverable and available evidence. Preserve an accepted brief, concept, script, or edit when it remains suitable.
A review starts at review. A production handoff starts from the approved script; it does not force new concepts.
For creation, use only the missing stages below. Record skipped stages and the accepted artifact that replaces each.

| Stage | Run when | Required input → output | Gate before advancing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Strategic brief](pitch-film-studio.md#strategic-brief) | Audience, promise, evidence, or destination is unresolved | Supplied facts → shared brief and claim sources | Resolve material contradictions; record the user's accepted direction |
| [Three concepts](pitch-film-studio.md#three-concepts) | A direction is needed or explicitly reopened | Brief revision → three alternatives and one selected concept | Selection fits the brief; unsupported promises remain blocked |
| [Script](pitch-film-studio.md#causal-script) | Writing or changing the story | Brief and concept revisions → six to eight causal beats, timed A/V script, exact transcript | Read-through, truth and silent-comprehension review; record approval or unresolved gaps |
| [Production handoff](pitch-film-studio.md#production-handoff) | Capturing, generating, directing, or editing | Approved script and asset audit → director/editor packet | Rights, actual proof and selected-tool authority exist before their dependent action |
| [Evidence review](pitch-film-studio.md#evidence-and-quality-review) | Reviewing a script, edit, or export | Exact revision and available artifacts → findings and evidence status | Fix material defects; inspect exported files before accepting production |

## Strategic brief

Separate the audience from the on-screen protagonist. The buyer may be a parent while a grandparent drives the scene.
State what the audience should think, feel, and do after one viewing.
Write one single-minded proposition: one audience benefit supported by one visible mechanism.

Record the audience's current problem, customer language, main objection, reason to believe, destination, duration, and call to action.
Keep a customer's exact words with their permitted source. Label invented language as a hypothesis, never a testimonial.
For each claim, retain its source path, revision, supported wording, limits, and status: verified, assumed, simulated, or blocked.
Separate an approved promise from evidence that the product performs it.
Omit or qualify unsupported outcomes, pricing, endorsements, comparisons, and urgency.

Stop dependent writing when conflicting requirements change the promise or intended action.
Use a declared assumption for a harmless missing detail. Obtain a direction decision when the choice materially changes scope.
Approval already present in the conversation can satisfy that decision; do not require another ceremony.

## Three concepts

Create exactly three genuinely different concepts before selecting one, when this stage applies.
For each, state the premise, protagonist, tension, story mechanism, proof moment, ending, and production constraint.
Change the dramatic mechanism and point of view, not only the hook, setting, color, or adjective.
Examples of mechanisms include a problem resolved through action, an objection tested, and a discovery through an object's journey.
Do not require those three mechanisms for every film.

Compare each concept against the proposition, available proof, objections, audience language, duration, and production feasibility.
Explain the selected direction and why the others lose. Record who selected it and the brief revision.
If selection is delegated, recommend within that authority. If no concept fits, repair the brief or expose the evidence gap.

## Causal script

Write six to eight beats. Each beat changes the situation because of the previous beat or forces the next action.
Use “because” and “therefore” to test connections. Cut a beat that merely adds another attractive feature.
Show the protagonist trying, encountering tension, acting, seeing a supported change, and reaching the intended next action.

Write the timed audio/visual script with these separate columns:

| Time in/out | Beat and causal link | Visible action/camera | Exact UI or chat text | Exact voiceover/dialogue | Sound/silence | Claim/proof source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start–end | What changes, because/therefore | Capture or edit instruction | Literal text, speaker and order | Literal spoken words, speaker | Cue or deliberate silence | Source revision and evidence status |

Use one exact transcript as the wording owner. Give each UI, chat, caption, voiceover and dialogue line an ID.
Reference those IDs from the script and editor packet; do not maintain divergent paraphrases.
Chat messages are on-screen text. Voiceover is spoken audio. Mark “none” explicitly when either is absent.
Identify captions that reproduce speech and overlays that add information. Keep their reading demand visible.
For managed app UI strings, reference existing copy-deck keys rather than inventing replacement product text.

Perform a timed read-through at the intended delivery pace, including pauses, UI reading, transitions and the final card.
Record who read, script revision, actual elapsed time, and overruns. Word-count arithmetic is an estimate, not a read-through.
Until an actual read occurs, label slots provisional and timing unverified. Trim or extend by decision; never silently accelerate every line.

Review the silent sequence: can the audience identify the problem, action, supported change, and next step without audio?
An independent script review can predict confusion. Actual human comprehension requires a real viewing and recorded answers.
Do not claim silent comprehension from a text-only script or infer it from captions being present.
Revise in order: story, factual truth, timing, then polish. Recheck downstream artifacts after each material change.

## Production handoff

Audit existing footage, screenshots, recordings, audio, logos, fonts, project files, and exports before rebuilding assets.
Record usable sections, missing shots, source revisions, rights, and defects in the existing asset packet.
Reuse accepted assets where they meet the current brief; request only the missing or failed material.

Give the director/editor the brief revision, selected concept, beat sheet, exact transcript revision, timed script, and claim sources.
Add shot order, framing, performance notes, transitions, sound, text layers, aspect ratio, duration, safe areas and export requirements.
Reference existing manifest asset IDs and inputs. Add edit decisions and required pickups there; do not create a parallel asset manifest.
Record unresolved decisions and a clear return condition for each blocked shot.

Use live demo capture for actual product proof. Retain the original recording and identify the demonstrated product revision and flow.
Label simulated flows clearly in the film while they appear, and in the packet. Simulation is not evidence of implemented behavior.
A camera move, generated screen, or composited chat cannot prove that the real product executed the action.
Keep UI text, chat, captions, numbers, and logos deterministic and editable in the selected editor or composition project.
Inspect text at delivery size; do not ask a generative video model to reproduce exact interface lettering.

Optional Higgsfield atmosphere shots apply only when explicitly selected, available, and already authorized.
Use short supporting shots; retain character, wardrobe, location, lighting and camera continuity references across shots.
Keep product proof separate from atmosphere. Load the selected tool's current guidance only when that action is requested.
A tool mention grants no spend, account setup, credential access, installation, fallback or generation authority.
Other tool-specific instructions, including Remotion or capture tools, remain conditional on the selected production operation.

For jitter, inspect a ten-second sample containing the failure before changing the whole edit.
Compare source and export frame timing, repeated or missing frames, playback load, capture rate and timeline settings.
Distinguish dropped source frames from an export cadence or playback problem.
A constant-frame-rate export cannot restore missing source motion; repair or recapture the source when that is the cause.
Record the diagnosis and compare the repaired sample before processing the remaining film.

Finish in the existing editor. Preserve editable layers, source links, versioned project files, and a reproducible export specification.
Verify the actual exported files: open and play them, inspect start/end, sound sync, text, labels, crops, duration and destination requirements.
Check representative frames and the troublesome motion sample. A render log or timeline preview alone is insufficient.

## Evidence and quality review

Review the exact brief, transcript, script or export revision. Use an independent reviewer who did not write that revision.
Return each material finding with its beat/time, conflicting source, consequence, and required correction.
After repair, review the changed revision and affected guarantees before carrying forward acceptance.

| Evidence class | What it can establish | What remains separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterministic contract checks | Required fields, time arithmetic, IDs, local links and hashes resolve | Story quality, factual truth, actual playback and viewer understanding |
| Independent script review | Source conflicts, predicted confusion, weak causality, objection and wording defects | Human comprehension or commercial performance |
| Timed read-through | Actual spoken delivery and pauses for that script and reader | Film pacing, screen readability and audience comprehension |
| Export inspection | Observed playback, text, sync and file properties in the inspected environment | Broader device coverage or audience response |
| Human comprehension | Recorded answers from actual viewers under stated conditions | Conversion, retention, revenue or general population claims |
| Commercial measurement | Observed outcomes with period, sample, denominator and attribution limits | Causation or savings without a suitable comparison |

Ask actual viewers to describe the problem, product action, reason to believe, and next step after viewing.
Record what they said, viewing conditions and consent. If no viewers participated, report human comprehension as unverified.
Never invent rendered footage, audience feedback, editing time or revision savings.
Compare time or revision costs only with observed, comparable baselines; unknown values remain unknown.
Return the artifacts, sources, checks run, findings, unresolved production gates, and remaining human or commercial validation.
